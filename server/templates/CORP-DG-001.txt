## 1. Purpose and Scope

[DATA_CONTROLLER_NAME] ("the Organisation") is committed to protecting the personal data it holds and processes in accordance with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018. This policy applies to all employees, contractors, agency workers, volunteers, and third parties who process personal data on behalf of the Organisation.

This policy sets out the Organisation's approach to data protection, the obligations of those who handle personal data, and the procedures in place to ensure compliance.

## 3. Data Protection Principles

All personal data processed by [DATA_CONTROLLER_NAME] must be:

1. **Processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently** — individuals must be informed about how their data is used.
2. **Collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes** — data must not be used in ways incompatible with the original purpose.
3. **Adequate, relevant, and limited** — only data that is necessary for the stated purpose should be collected and retained.
4. **Accurate and kept up to date** — reasonable steps must be taken to correct or erase inaccurate data without delay.
5. **Retained no longer than necessary** — see the Data Retention and Disposal Policy (CORP-DG-003) for retention schedules.
6. **Processed securely** — appropriate technical and organisational measures must be in place to protect personal data.

---

## 4. Lawful Basis for Processing

The Organisation relies on the following lawful bases for processing personal data, as defined under Article 6 UK GDPR:

- **Contract performance** — processing necessary for the performance of a contract with the data subject.
- **Legal obligation** — processing required to comply with a statutory or regulatory obligation.
- **Legitimate interests** — processing necessary for the Organisation's legitimate business interests, where those interests are not overridden by the rights and freedoms of the data subject.
- **Consent** — where no other lawful basis applies, explicit consent will be sought and recorded.

Where the Organisation processes special category data (as defined under Article 9 UK GDPR), an additional condition under Schedule 1 of the Data Protection Act 2018 must also be identified and documented. The Data Protection Officer ([DPO_NAME]) maintains a Record of Processing Activities (ROPA) which documents the lawful basis for each processing activity.

## 5. Data Subject Rights

[DATA_CONTROLLER_NAME] respects and facilitates the following rights under UK GDPR:

- **Right of access** — individuals may request a copy of personal data held about them (see CORP-DG-004).
- **Right to rectification** — individuals may request correction of inaccurate or incomplete data.
- **Right to erasure** — individuals may request deletion of their data where no lawful basis for retention exists.
- **Right to restriction of processing** — individuals may ask the Organisation to pause processing in certain circumstances.
- **Right to data portability** — individuals may request their data in a structured, machine-readable format.
- **Right to object** — individuals may object to processing based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing.
- **Rights related to automated decision-making** — individuals have the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions that produce significant effects.

All rights requests should be directed to [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] and must be actioned within one calendar month of receipt. The DPO ([DPO_NAME]) oversees fulfilment of all data subject rights requests.

## 6. International Data Transfers

The Organisation will not transfer personal data outside the UK unless one of the following safeguards is in place:

- An adequacy decision issued by the UK Secretary of State.
- Appropriate safeguards such as UK International Data Transfer Agreements (IDTAs) or UK Addenda to Standard Contractual Clauses.
- A recognised derogation under Article 49 UK GDPR applies.

Any proposed international transfers must be assessed and approved by [DPO_NAME] prior to commencement. Technical queries regarding transfer mechanisms should be directed to [IT_EMAIL].

---

## 7. Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)

A Data Protection Impact Assessment must be conducted before commencing any processing activity that is likely to result in a high risk to individuals' rights and freedoms. This includes, but is not limited to:

- Large-scale processing of special category data.
- Systematic monitoring of individuals.
- Use of new technologies with significant privacy implications.
- Profiling or automated decision-making with legal or significant effects.

DPIAs are co-ordinated by [DPO_NAME]. Where a DPIA indicates a high residual risk that cannot be mitigated, the Organisation will consult [PRIMARY_REGULATOR] prior to processing.

## 8. Records of Processing Activities (ROPA)

[DATA_CONTROLLER_NAME] maintains a ROPA in accordance with Article 30 UK GDPR. The ROPA documents:

- Name and contact details of the Data Controller and DPO.
- Purposes of each processing activity.
- Categories of data subjects and personal data.
- Recipients and international transfers.
- Retention periods.
- Security measures in place.

The ROPA is maintained by [DPO_NAME] and is available for inspection by [PRIMARY_REGULATOR] upon request. Department heads are responsible for notifying [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] of any new processing activities so the ROPA can be updated.

## 9. Data Security

The Organisation implements appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect personal data against unauthorised access, loss, destruction, or disclosure. These measures include data encryption, access controls, and secure disposal. For technical security requirements, refer to the IT Security Policy suite (CORP-IT-001 to CORP-IT-005). Security incidents must be reported immediately to [IT_EMAIL] and [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL].
