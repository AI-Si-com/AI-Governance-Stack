## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy establishes the framework for identifying, reporting, investigating, containing, and learning from incidents involving artificial intelligence systems operated by [ORG_NAME]. Effective AI incident response is essential to limit harm to individuals, protect [ORG_NAME]'s operations and reputation, and fulfil legal and regulatory notification obligations.

This policy applies to all AI systems deployed by [ORG_NAME], including [AI_TOOLS_LIST], and to all personnel involved in their operation, oversight, or governance. It must be read alongside the AI Bias and Fairness Policy (AISI-BIAS-001), the AI and Privacy Policy (AISI-PRIV-001), and [ORG_NAME]'s broader Information Security Incident Management Policy.

All AI incidents must be recorded and tracked in [INCIDENT_TRACKING_SYSTEM].

---

## 2. AI Incident Categories

An AI incident is any event where an AI system produces an output, behaves in a manner, or is subject to an external act that causes or has the potential to cause harm. [ORG_NAME] classifies AI incidents into the following categories:

| Category | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Erroneous Output** | Model produces incorrect, misleading, or low-quality outputs | Incorrect predictions, hallucinated content, corrupted outputs |
| **Bias or Discrimination** | Model produces outputs that are unfair or discriminatory | Disparate rejection rates, protected characteristic proxying |
| **Privacy Breach** | AI system discloses, exposes, or misuses personal data | Training data extraction, PII in AI outputs, unauthorised inference |
| **Security Compromise** | AI system is subject to adversarial attack or manipulation | Data poisoning, prompt injection, model theft, adversarial inputs |
| **Availability Failure** | AI system becomes unavailable, degraded, or behaves erratically | Model drift, infrastructure failure, dependency outage |
| **Misuse** | AI system is used for a purpose outside its approved scope | Unauthorised use cases, policy violations by users |
| **Third-Party Incident** | An AI vendor or supplier reports or causes an incident | Vendor data breach, supplier model compromise |

---

## 3. Severity Levels

All AI incidents must be assigned a severity level upon initial triage:

| Severity | Criteria | Initial Response Target |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 – Critical** | Significant harm to individuals; regulatory breach; widespread system compromise; data breach involving personal data | Immediate (within 1 hour) |
| **P2 – High** | Material adverse impact on individuals or operations; potential regulatory notification requirement; suspected bias affecting protected groups | Within 4 hours |
| **P3 – Medium** | Localised impact; degraded performance; isolated erroneous outputs; potential policy violation | Within 24 hours |
| **P4 – Low** | Minor anomaly; no immediate harm; informational | Within 5 business days |

Severity may be escalated at any point as new information becomes available. Downgrading of severity requires approval from [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD].

## 5. Incident Detection and Reporting

### 5.1 Reporting Obligations

All employees and contractors must report suspected AI incidents as soon as they are identified. Reports must be made to [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD] via [INCIDENT_TRACKING_SYSTEM] or, in an emergency, by direct contact to [IT_EMAIL].

A suspected AI incident must be reported wherever a person reasonably believes that an AI system has:

- Produced an output that has caused or could cause harm to any person
- Behaved in a manner inconsistent with its documented design
- Been accessed, manipulated, or exploited by an unauthorised party
- Generated, disclosed, or processed personal data in an unintended manner

### 5.2 No-Blame Reporting Culture

[ORG_NAME] is committed to a no-blame incident reporting culture. Employees who report AI incidents in good faith will not face disciplinary consequences for doing so. Prompt reporting is encouraged and valued.

## 6. Response Procedures

### 6.1 Phase 1 – Identification and Triage (0–4 hours for P1/P2)

Upon receipt of an incident report, [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD] must:

1. Acknowledge receipt and log the incident in [INCIDENT_TRACKING_SYSTEM]
2. Assign an initial severity rating
3. Notify the relevant AIRT members
4. Conduct initial scoping to establish: what AI system is affected; what outputs or data are involved; who has been or may be affected; and what immediate actions have already been taken

### 6.2 Phase 2 – Containment

Containment actions must be taken as rapidly as possible to limit ongoing harm. Options include:

- Suspending the affected AI system or disabling specific functions
- Revoking access to affected systems or data
- Isolating affected infrastructure components
- Implementing manual override or substitution processes
- Issuing interim guidance to staff on not acting on AI outputs pending investigation

Containment decisions must be documented in [INCIDENT_TRACKING_SYSTEM] and approved by [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD].

### 6.3 Phase 3 – Investigation

The AIRT must conduct a structured investigation to establish:

- Root cause of the incident (data, model, infrastructure, human error, external attack)
- Timeline of events
- Full scope of impact: systems, data, individuals, and business processes affected
- Whether any regulatory notification obligations have been triggered

Technical investigation is led by [IT_EMAIL]; legal and regulatory assessment is led by [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL].

### 6.4 Phase 4 – Notification

#### Internal Notification

[CEO_NAME] must be briefed on all P1 and P2 incidents within four hours of severity assignment. The Board or equivalent governance body must be notified of P1 incidents within 24 hours.

#### Regulatory Notification

| Trigger | Notification Body | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Personal data breach likely to result in risk to individuals | Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) | Within 72 hours of becoming aware |
| Breach involving likely high risk to individuals | Affected data subjects | Without undue delay |
| Incident affecting regulated activities | [PRIMARY_REGULATOR] | Per regulatory requirements |

All regulatory notifications must be coordinated by [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] and approved by [CEO_NAME] before submission.

### 6.5 Phase 5 – Recovery

Recovery involves restoring the AI system to full, safe operation. Recovery must not occur until:

- Root cause has been identified and remediated
- Appropriate testing (including bias and security testing where relevant) has been completed
- [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD] has formally approved return to service
- The recovery plan has been documented in [INCIDENT_TRACKING_SYSTEM]

---

## 7. Post-Incident Review

A Post-Incident Review (PIR) must be completed for all P1 and P2 incidents, and for P3 incidents at the discretion of [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD]. The PIR must:

- Be completed within 10 business days of incident closure
- Document root cause, contributing factors, and timeline
- Identify lessons learned
- Produce a set of documented remediation actions with owners and deadlines
- Be shared with [CEO_NAME] and [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL]

PIR outcomes must be reviewed at the next AI governance cycle and tracked to completion.
