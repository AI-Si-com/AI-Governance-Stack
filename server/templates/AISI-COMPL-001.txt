## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy establishes [ORG_NAME]'s framework for identifying, monitoring, and complying with the regulatory and legal obligations applicable to its use of artificial intelligence systems, including [AI_TOOLS_LIST]. The AI regulatory landscape is evolving rapidly; this policy provides a structured approach to staying current with legal developments and embedding compliance into AI governance.

This policy applies to all AI deployments within [ORG_NAME] and to all personnel with responsibility for AI governance, procurement, operation, or oversight. It should be read alongside all other AI Governance policies (AISI series) and the organisation's broader Legal and Regulatory Compliance Policy.

## 2. Applicable Regulatory Framework

[ORG_NAME]'s AI activities are subject to the following regulatory landscape:

### 2.1 UK AI Governance Framework

The UK does not currently have a single, comprehensive AI-specific statute. Instead, AI regulation operates through a cross-sectoral framework based on the following:

- **UK Government AI Regulation White Paper (2023) principles:** Safety, security, fairness, transparency and explainability, accountability, contestability and redress. [ORG_NAME] adopts these principles as minimum standards for all AI systems
- **ICO guidance on AI and data protection:** Including guidance on explaining AI decisions, fairness in AI, and AI auditing. All AI processing of personal data must comply with this guidance
- **FCA/PRA (where applicable):** Expectations on model risk management, algorithmic fairness, and explainability in regulated financial services contexts
- **MHRA, NHS, CQC (where applicable):** Sector-specific requirements for AI used in medical devices, clinical decision support, or health services

### 2.2 EU AI Act (Awareness and Preparedness)

Although the UK is not subject to EU law, [ORG_NAME] recognises that:

- UK-based organisations that supply AI systems to EU customers, process data of EU individuals, or operate within the EU single market may be subject to the EU AI Act
- The EU AI Act establishes a risk-based classification of AI systems (Unacceptable, High, Limited, Minimal risk) with corresponding obligations
- High-risk AI categories under the EU AI Act include: employment and workers management, education, essential private and public services, law enforcement, biometric identification, and critical infrastructure

[AI_TOOLS_LIST] must be assessed against EU AI Act risk categories by [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] where any EU nexus exists.

## 3. Risk-Based Compliance Classification

[ORG_NAME] applies a risk-based approach to AI compliance, consistent with emerging regulatory frameworks. All AI systems must be classified into the following risk tiers and managed accordingly:

| Risk Tier | Description | Compliance Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Prohibited** | AI systems that pose unacceptable risks (e.g. social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance in public spaces) | Must not be deployed under any circumstances |
| **High-risk** | AI systems that pose significant risk of harm to individuals or society | Full AISI policy suite compliance; DPIA; AIA; regular audit; regulatory notification where required |
| **Limited-risk** | AI systems with transparency obligations (e.g. chatbots, deepfakes) | Transparency disclosures required; periodic review |
| **Minimal-risk** | Low-risk AI applications | Baseline governance; included in annual review |

Risk classification decisions must be made by [AI_APPROVAL_AUTHORITY] and documented in the AI System Register.

## 4. Compliance Monitoring

[ORG_NAME] maintains active compliance monitoring across AI regulatory developments. [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] is responsible for:

- Subscribing to regulatory publications, consultation papers, and guidance updates from [PRIMARY_REGULATOR] and other relevant bodies
- Tracking parliamentary bills and statutory instruments affecting AI governance
- Monitoring ICO enforcement actions, regulatory fines, and published investigations relevant to AI
- Reviewing EU AI Act implementation milestones and applicability assessments
- Attending or reviewing output from relevant industry bodies (e.g. AI Safety Institute, Centre for Data Ethics and Innovation, BSI AI standards programme)

A compliance intelligence summary must be prepared by [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] and presented to [CEO_NAME] on a quarterly basis.

---

## 5. Compliance Assessment Cycle

All AI systems must undergo a formal compliance assessment at least annually, and at any of the following trigger events:

- A new AI system is introduced or an existing system is materially changed
- New legislation, regulation, or regulatory guidance is published affecting AI in [ORG_NAME]'s sector
- [PRIMARY_REGULATOR] issues an enforcement action, consultation, or significant guidance relevant to [ORG_NAME]'s AI activities
- A regulatory breach or near-miss is identified

Compliance assessments must be documented and retained for a minimum of five years. Material compliance gaps must be escalated to [CEO_NAME] and remediated within agreed timescales.

## 7. Regulatory Engagement

[ORG_NAME] will engage proactively and cooperatively with [PRIMARY_REGULATOR] and other relevant authorities. This includes:

- Responding to regulatory consultations on AI governance where [ORG_NAME] has relevant expertise or interests
- Participating in regulatory sandboxes, pilots, or innovation programmes where appropriate
- Notifying [PRIMARY_REGULATOR] of AI incidents or significant AI deployments where required by applicable rules
- Maintaining open and transparent communication in the event of a regulatory investigation

All regulatory correspondence relating to AI must be managed by [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] and copied to [CEO_NAME]. No individual should communicate with a regulator regarding AI governance matters without prior coordination with [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL].

## 9. Breach Identification and Escalation

Where a potential or actual breach of applicable AI regulation is identified:

1. The identifying employee must immediately notify [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL]
2. [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] must assess the nature and severity of the breach within 24 hours
3. [CEO_NAME] must be notified of any breach carrying material regulatory, financial, or reputational risk
4. Where the breach requires regulatory notification, this must be coordinated by [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] within applicable timeframes
5. Root cause analysis and remediation must be documented and tracked to closure
