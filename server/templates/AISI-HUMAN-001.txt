## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy sets out [ORG_NAME]'s requirements for meaningful human oversight of AI systems. Human oversight is a non-negotiable component of responsible AI governance: it ensures that AI operates within acceptable boundaries, that errors can be identified and corrected, and that individuals retain the ability to challenge and override automated outputs.

This policy applies to all AI systems deployed across [ORG_NAME] and to all employees who operate, supervise, or are affected by AI-assisted processes. It applies with particular force wherever AI influences decisions affecting individuals or involves significant operational or reputational risk. Departments in scope include [DEPARTMENTS_LIST].

[IF AI_AUTOMATED_DECISIONS == "Yes" THEN]
[ORG_NAME] operates AI systems that produce automated or semi-automated decisions. This policy establishes the minimum standards for human review of those decisions in compliance with UK GDPR Article 22 and [ORG_NAME]'s AI governance framework.
[END IF]

## 2. Principles of Human Oversight

[ORG_NAME] adopts the following principles to guide human oversight of AI:

- **Genuine oversight:** Human review must be substantive and informed, not merely procedural. Reviewers must have the time, information, and authority to challenge AI outputs
- **Proportionality:** The intensity of oversight must be commensurate with the risk and significance of the decision being supported
- **Accountability:** Every AI-assisted decision must have a named human accountable for it
- **Correctability:** Humans must always retain the ability to override, correct, or reverse AI outputs
- **No automation by default:** Automation of a decision or process should be the result of a deliberate governance decision, not a default or default-forward setting

## 3. Minimum Human Review Requirements

The following minimum human review requirements apply based on decision type and risk level:

### 3.1 High-Risk Decisions (Mandatory Human Approval)

The following categories of decision must never be finalised solely on the basis of AI output. A qualified human reviewer must review the AI output, consider available contextual information, and make the final decision:

- Employment decisions (recruitment, promotion, performance assessment, disciplinary action, redundancy)
- Credit, lending, or financial access decisions affecting individuals
- Medical, clinical, or health-related decisions
- Decisions affecting welfare, benefits, or housing
- Legal or compliance determinations with significant consequences
- Any decision where the affected individual has exercised a right to human review under UK GDPR Article 22

The reviewer must document their reasoning independently of the AI output and retain a record of their decision.

### 3.2 Medium-Risk Decisions (Human Review with Override Capability)

For medium-risk decisions, AI outputs may be used as a primary input, but a human reviewer must:

- Be presented with the AI output alongside the key factors that influenced it
- Have access to the underlying data and the ability to query the reasoning
- Have an accessible mechanism to override the AI recommendation before the decision is finalised
- Be alerted to low-confidence or out-of-distribution AI outputs

### 3.3 Low-Risk Decisions (Monitoring and Exception Review)

For low-risk automated processes, continuous oversight by a designated individual is required, together with:

- Regular sampling and review of AI outputs
- Automated alerts for outputs outside defined confidence thresholds
- A clear escalation pathway for exceptions

## 4. Override Mechanisms

Every AI system deployed by [ORG_NAME] must incorporate a functioning override mechanism that enables authorised users to:

- Reject or modify an AI output before it takes effect
- Substitute an alternative human judgment for an AI recommendation
- Suspend an AI function pending investigation

Override events must be logged automatically, recording the user identity, timestamp, the AI output that was overridden, and the override decision. Override logs must be accessible to [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD] and reviewed as part of the audit process (AISI-AUDIT-001).

Disabling or circumventing an override mechanism without express authorisation from [AI_APPROVAL_AUTHORITY] constitutes a policy breach.

---

## 5. Human-in-the-Loop Processes for High-Risk Decisions

For all high-risk AI applications, [ORG_NAME] must maintain formally documented Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) processes specifying:

- The point in the process at which human review occurs
- The information made available to the reviewer
- The qualifications and training required of reviewers
- The expected time to be allocated to the review
- The documentation to be completed by the reviewer
- How conflicts between human judgment and AI output are resolved and recorded

HITL process documentation must be held in the AI System Register by [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD] and reviewed annually or following any material change to the AI system or underlying process.

## 6. Automation Bias Prevention

Automation bias — the tendency to over-rely on AI outputs and to discount conflicting human judgment — poses a significant risk to the quality of human oversight. [ORG_NAME] requires the following measures to counteract automation bias:

### 6.1 Process Design

- Review interfaces must present the AI recommendation as one input among several, not as a default answer requiring justification to deviate from
- Where feasible, reviewers should be prompted to form their own initial assessment before seeing the AI output
- Systems must display AI confidence levels and flag outputs where the model's confidence is low

### 6.2 Training

All employees who review AI outputs as part of their role must complete automation bias awareness training as part of the AI literacy programme (AISI-TRAIN-001). Training must cover the nature and risks of automation bias and practical techniques for maintaining independent judgment.

### 6.3 Monitoring for Automation Bias

[AI_INCIDENT_LEAD] must monitor override rates as a proxy indicator of automation bias. Abnormally low override rates in high-risk decision contexts must be investigated and addressed.

## 7. Accountability Chain

For every AI-assisted decision process, [ORG_NAME] must document a clear accountability chain identifying:

- The AI system involved and its owner
- The role responsible for human review at each stage
- The senior leader accountable for the process
- The escalation path for concerns or disputes

Accountability chain documentation must cover all departments using AI: [DEPARTMENTS_LIST]. This documentation is maintained by [AI_INCIDENT_LEAD] and reviewed by [COMPLIANCE_EMAIL] annually.

---

## 8. Affected Individual Rights

Where an individual is affected by an AI-assisted decision, [ORG_NAME] will:

- Inform them that AI was used in the decision-making process, upon request
- Provide a meaningful explanation of the factors considered
- Offer a human review of the decision upon request, conducted by a reviewer with authority to reach a different outcome
- Not penalise individuals for requesting human review
